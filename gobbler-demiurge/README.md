# Gobbler demiurge: the bird first

A page that boots with the bird alone: the Gobbler 3 Deep head and knowledge graph, the Sun in its chest that beats a replay of real validator runs, a Dyson ribcage of the 364 ontology classes, plumage and a tail fan modelled in Blender, weaving shuttles and a slow cinematic tour. Fly down the rail (keys 4 to 7) and the camera leaves the bird for Earth and the Moon, then the village Aurelsbrunn on it, its plaza and a villager 1.75 m tall, with a scale bar that says what the screen is showing in village metres. Earth, the Moon, the village and the villagers are modelled in Blender and load only when the camera comes near. It is a **new folder on top of `gobbler-v4/`**, which is untouched: the Deep scene is used byte for byte, and the v4 hover layer runs on top of it unchanged.

Built so far (plan steps): 1 honest loader and gzip delivery, 2 the bird-first page in the locked look, 3 the world as lazy Inlines and ladder stops 4 to 7 (done with a new Blender village, not v2's; since then a fidelity pass: the village's light baked in Cycles, an atmosphere shader, a fountain, clothed villagers), 5 feathers made in Blender, 6 motion (tour, seeded drift, preening wave, shuttles), 7 the heartbeat (heart only), 8 the shell tenders as H-Anim humanoids made in Blender, 10 sound (silent until `M`: a music bed for the cosmos and one for the village that cross as you fly down, and the Sun's heartbeat thumped on the tape that drives its swell), 11 the picture-judge loop (measured: [docs/PICTURE-JUDGE.md](docs/PICTURE-JUDGE.md)). Step 9 (the tail fan lit by real glTF, USD and video exports) stays **simulated** on purpose: a real export path is the ultimate vision, the plumes are tagged accordingly. Not built: 12 trailer, the flywheel growing the village, the eclipse stop.

```
make v4-data          # (once) the Deep build and X_ITE 16.5.1, hash-checked
make demiurge-data    # compose the bird scene, gzip it, validate with the fork       -> gobbler-demiurge/data/bird/ (not committed)
make feathers         # (only to remake the feathers; needs a Python with bpy) model + bake + export in Blender -> assets/feathers/ (committed)
make tender           # (same) the H-Anim shell tender -> assets/tender/ and its three villager outfits -> assets/villager/ (committed)
make world            # (same) Earth, clouds, Moon and the village -> assets/earth/, assets/village/ (committed); make demiurge-data then writes the five lazy files (planet, clouds, Moon, village, villagers) and world.json into data/bird/
make test-demiurge    # layer tests (python only)
make demiurge         # serve the repo root: http://127.0.0.1:8791/gobbler-demiurge/index.html
make e2e-demiurge     # the live page in Chromium (serve first, same PORT)
```

Parameters: `?ui=none|min` (stills are `?ui=none`), `?view=face|ribcage|heart|earth|village|plaza|villager|feather|establishing`, `?seed=N` (the camera's drift and the flights' bows; the same seed repeats a take, `?seed=0` turns the randomness off, default 4), `?look=gold` (the gold-tinted knowledge graph, arm C of the picture-judge test), `?tourat=N` (start the tour N seconds in; a test hook: the dive to the village comes at 65 s), and the v4 ones (`?hover=<entity>&tier=2|3`, `?tune=1`, `?glass=1`).

## Blender pipeline for the feathers

`tools/blender_feather.py` builds each feather in Blender's Python (bmesh): a quill, a vane on a 24 by 3 grid with cup, twist and a small seeded wobble (so the quads stop being flat), triangulated and flat shaded. It lights a white copy with a golden-hour rig (a warm sun lamp at 24 degrees plus a cool multiple-scattering sky), bakes direct and indirect light into corner colours with Cycles, sets the exposure so the 92nd percentile facet sits at 0.92, and exports through x3d-blender-io as X3D 4.0, one file per variant. `tools/feathers.py` reads those files back and `layers.py` places them with a Transform per plume (rotation from the plume's own axes, scale from its length and width). The page build itself does not need Blender. `assets/feathers/receipt.json` records the Blender version, the exporter commit, the script's sha256 and the file hashes.

## Blender pipeline for the tenders

`tools/blender_tender.py` builds an armature of 18 joints in Blender (the lowest H-Anim 2.0 articulation level as I remember it: humanoid_root, sacroiliac, vl5, vt6, vc4, skullbase, and left and right shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle; **the joint list is from memory and has not been checked against ISO/IEC 19774-1**) and one faceted skin mesh weighted rigidly to those joints, with per-face colours from a fixed key light (gold, cool shadows). x3d-blender-io exports the armature plus its skinned mesh as an `HAnimHumanoid` (version 2.0) with an `HAnimJoint` per bone, `skinCoord`, per-joint `skinCoordIndex` and `skinCoordWeight`, and one skin Shape. `tools/tender.py` reads that file and hands out copies with a prefix on every DEF; the PhysicalMaterial is dropped so the baked face colours draw unlit like the feathers. The composed scene gets one more head statement, `<component name='HAnim' level='1'/>`, so the compose step now makes three insertions (the test checks all three and that the rest is the Deep scene byte for byte). A bug caught on the way: the first export weighted only the torso joints (new bmesh vertices have no valid index until `index_update`), so arms and legs did not bend; it shows as 0 skin indices on the limb joints in the file.

## Blender pipeline for the villagers

`tools/blender_tender.py --look a|b|c --out assets/villager` builds the tender's armature and the same kinds of parts (so the same 18 joints, the same object names and the same walk cycles carry over) and colours them as clothes and skin instead of gold: shirt, trousers, boots, skin and hair, the joint caps as pads in the cloth, and one thing per look that shows in a silhouette (a fall of hair, a brimmed hat, a basket). A run without `--look` is untouched: it writes the same `tender.x3d` byte for byte (sha256 573d5a79...). The light is the tender's fixed key (warm sunlit facets, cool shaded ones), not a Cycles bake: a walker turns round the plaza, so a baked direction would be right at one place on its lane only. `tools/tender.py` hands the copies out with their own DEF prefixes (`hanim_T3` to `hanim_T5`) and `tools/world_layer.py` writes `data/bird/villagers.x3d`: three bodies shared by USE among twelve walkers, with their walk cycles and orbits (interpolators and ROUTEs, no Script).

## Blender pipeline for the world

`tools/blender_earth.py` and `tools/blender_village.py` build the world in Blender's Python (bmesh) and export it through x3d-blender-io (commit d9bbf91, Blender 5.2.2 from the `bpy` wheel): the same hand as the feathers and the tenders. The planet's colours come from a formula and the Sun's direction. **The village's light is baked in Cycles** (CPU, 384 samples; the whole script, modelling, bake and export, takes about 13 s on a multi-core CPU): a sun lamp 55 degrees up from azimuth 220 (warm, strength 5, a soft disc of 6 degrees; the real Heart would stand straight overhead, but then every shape would lose its shadow side) and a Sky Texture with multiple scattering (sun disc off, strength 0.25, its own sun at 26 degrees: a probe on a flat plane showed that a sky of strength 1.0 lights it about 2.6 times as strongly as the key at 5, mean luminance 3.4 against 1.3, so a sky of 8, the second value tried after a first 0.9, would have drowned it; at 0.25 the sky gives about two thirds of the key's light). Shadows and bounces are counted: each face carries its albedo in a vertex colour that the bake's material reads, so the bounce off a wall is the colour of the wall (the intended effect is that the cream walls warm the paving and the pines cool the grass; it was not isolated or measured). The bake (`render.bake.target = 'VERTEX_COLORS'`, DIFFUSE, direct and indirect, no colour pass) is the light that arrives at each face corner. Afterwards, in Python: the exposure puts the 70th percentile ground corner at 1.0 (so sunlit ground shows its albedo), a floor of cool light keeps the deepest shadow readable, the light is multiplied by each face's albedo, the sRGB curve is applied (X_ITE draws vertex colours unlit with no gamma) and then a soft shoulder from 0.8 on the display value, and lamps, windows and water keep a share of their own glow. The things are merged into four meshes (Ground, Buildings, Props, Trees), which the exporter writes as four Shapes with a `Color` per face corner. **The cost, said plainly:** every copy of a house stands in its own light, so the copies cannot share colours, and the exporter splits vertices on (vertex, uv, colour, normal): one vertex per corner and no DEF/USE for the lit meshes. The earlier village (fixed key light, 18 meshes, 90 copies by USE, about 3,450 faces drawn) was 192 KB raw and 26 KB gzipped; this one (9,790 faces) is 1.35 MB and 330 KB, about half of the growth from the lost sharing and the rest from the added geometry, and it loads only when the camera nears the village. `tools/village_spec.py` holds the numbers both scripts and the page share (scale, the Earth's place, the village's frame). `tools/world.py` then writes the page's four files: the PhysicalMaterial goes (the faces draw unlit, like the feathers), positions shorten to four decimals (three for the village) and colours to three, the exporter's navigation node and background go, a `ColorRGBA` whose alpha is 1 everywhere becomes a `Color`, and the Earth export splits into planet, clouds and Moon because the page animates them separately. `assets/earth/receipt.json` and `assets/village/receipt.json` record the Blender version, the exporter commit, the script's sha256 and the file hashes; the village's also the bake (samples, lamp, sky, exposure, light percentiles per mesh).

**A defect the validator caught, and a wrong message from it.** The first Earth export baked some colours above 1 (up to about 1.1 by the formula for the bright ice). The X3D schema keeps `ColorRGBA` within [0, 1], a renderer simply clamps, and the fork's XSD check failed the file. Its message was wrong: it said libxml2 had stopped at its 10,000,000-byte start-tag limit, but the longest attribute was 263 KB. Bisecting the colour array found the cause (the first 100 colours pass, the 101st, with channels at 1.0059 and 1.0511, fails). libxml2 2.14.6 reports "Internal error in XML Schema validation" instead of a pattern error when such a value sits near the end of an array of 12 colours or more, and the fork turns any such error into the size message. The script now clamps the colours, and all six world files validate (below). The fork's message is worth fixing; this repo does not touch the fork.

**Two more that the validator cannot see.** (1) At village scale the cloud shell (radius 1.028) hangs about 7 m above the ground, which is roof height. The file is valid and the shell is right for the planet; in the plaza frames it showed as big translucent sheets over the sky. The fix is in the data: no cloud within 25 degrees of the village (fading in by 38) and a soft per-corner alpha so a bank fades out at its edge. (2) The first atmosphere used X_ITE's `DepthMode` node, and X_ITE 16.5.1 itself said `Unknown node type 'DepthMode'` in this page (the scene declares no X_ITE component, which X_ITE needs for them): `DepthMode` and `BlendMode` are X_ITE extension nodes that are not in the X3D 4.0 schema (the fork's XSD check would refuse them too). The shell now relies on what X_ITE already does for alpha-blended shapes, which is not to write depth. Neither is a bug in the validator; both sit in the "valid is not the same as visible" column.

## The zoom ladder: Earth to a villager

Stops 4 to 7 use the same rail and keys as the bird's stops. What happens between them:

- **Lazy files.** `world.json` (1.9 KB) arrives after the first frame and fills in the four stops. The planet, clouds, Moon, village and villagers files stay unloaded (`Inline load='false'`) until a ProximitySensor says the camera is near (one box around the Earth, 16 units across, for the first three; one around the village site, 2.6 units, for the last two): its `isActive` goes through a `BooleanFilter` to the Inline's `set_load`, and the group is drawn only while the sensor is active. Nothing heavy loads at boot: the e2e test's first request list is the scene, `world.json`, the graph, the anchors and the receipt. The Ribcage and Heart stops are inside the Earth's box and the Heart stop (key 3) is inside the village's, so in the last end-to-end run all five files were requested during the flight to the Heart stop; at the Face none is.
- **The sway pauses.** The Deep scene's own slow sway (up to about 0.2 rad every 32 s about the pivot (0.72, -0.76)) moves the Sun by about 1.2 units. That is harmless at the Face and fatal at the village, which a camera 0.13 units away would lose. So at stops 4 to 7 the page pauses the sway (the TimeSensor's `pauseTime`), converts the stop's bird-frame coordinates through the frozen rotation, and flies there; any flight back to a bird stop, the Face chip or the tour resumes it.
- **The dive.** A flight that starts or ends at a world stop is geometric in its distance to the subject (an equal factor of zoom per second, eased at both ends, so more than four orders of magnitude of distance read as one smooth fall), with the camera's up vector blended into the village's local up, the near and far clip planes set every frame from that distance, and X_ITE's logarithmic depth buffer on while down there. Flights between bird stops keep the smoothstep with the seeded bow.
- **Scale, said plainly.** The village is 160 m across on a planet drawn 250 m in radius (0.1 scene units; one village metre is 4e-4 units). The real Earth has a radius of 6,371 km, so the village is about 25,000 times too large for its planet. If the 3.3-unit ribcage is the plan's 2.4 AU, one unit is about 0.73 AU, Earth is drawn about 1,700 times too large and the Moon about 85 times too far out. A scale bar at the foot of the page shows a bar and what it spans in village metres (200 m, 20 m, 2 m, 0.5 m as you fall), and the Earth, village, tether and villager tags say these numbers.

## Sound: silent until `M`

Two beds and a heart. `tools/audio.py` masters two ambient clips (from a text-to-music model: three seeds per zone, chosen on measures first, then by listening reviewers) to the same loudness, -26 LUFS, and encodes AAC at 96 kbit/s: `assets/audio/cosmos.m4a` (the bird, the Sun, the ribcage and out to the Earth) and `village.m4a` (from the orbit stop down to a villager), 400 KB each, with `manifest.json` beside them (prompts, seeds, loudness before and after, sha256; no vendor names). Every source clip began with the same click (the first 12 stereo frames are identical in all six clips, with large values at frames 0, 2, 3, 6 and 9, peak -1.4 dBFS, about 0.2 ms long); the tool cuts the first 10 ms. `src/sound.js` does the rest in WebAudio, and only after `M`, because a browser starts audio on a gesture and a page should not speak first:

- **The beds loop** by crossfading two overlapping copies of the clip (equal power, 4 s), so whatever a codec pads at the ends never shows. Both beds play; the mix is the cosine and the sine of how near the camera is to the village (all village by 0.01 of the bird's units from the site, all cosmos from 0.35; the orbit stop is 0.13 away, the plaza 0.006), so the two always sum to the same power.
- **The heart** is synthesized (a sine falling from 64 to 40 Hz with its octave above it, a softer second thump at the dub, and a short noise tick on a red beat, a defect found). It is thumped on the beat tape in `data/bird/heart.json`, which `build_bird.py` writes from the same list the visual swell is keyed on, and placed against the HeartClock's own phase, so a thump lands with the swell. Loudest at the Heart stop, half at the ribcage, a third at the Face, silent from orbit down (nothing is even scheduled there).
- **It fails soft.** A missing bed or a browser without WebAudio leaves the page silent and says why in a toast (only while the UI is on: `?ui=none` plays but never shows one). `visibilitychange` suspends the context in a hidden tab.

## Step 1 in this build: honest loader and gzip delivery

`src/boot.js` replaces the engine's fixed status text. Every stage names itself with bytes and seconds, and a failure prints the stage and the error text instead of a counter:

```
starting X_ITE → fetching the bird · 308 KB of 308 KB → parsing the scene · 1.37 MB of X3D → drawing the first frame → bird on screen
```

The scene ships as `gobbler4-bird.x3d.gz` and is inflated in the page (`DecompressionStream`); a published copy can carry base64 text of the gzip (`GD_CFG.enc = 'b64gz'`) for hosts that refuse binary. The graph (266 KB gzipped), the ontology block, the tag anchors and the validator receipt load **after the first frame**; the hover layer starts when the graph arrives. This ports the technique from the Constellation 2 version 3 fix (gzip on the wire, inflate in the page, a status line that says where it is); the code is new, because that fix lives in a packaged copy, not in this repo.

## Controls: four, as planned

UI level (`U`, none / minimal), Face (home view, key 1), Tour, and a right-edge rail of 8 zoom stops (keys 1 to 8), all live: 1 Face, 2 Dyson ribcage, 3 Heart, 4 Earth and Moon, 5 Village from orbit, 6 Plaza, 7 A villager, 8 A feather (stops 4 to 7 light up when `world.json` has arrived, a moment after the first frame). `T` toggles the tour and `Shift+T` opens the v4 tuning panel (the plan's key rule; the panel's own footer still says "T tune"). `Shift+F` is full screen; hold `H` hides everything; `M` switches sound on and off (silent to begin with, see Sound below). Lights out (`L`) is not wired. The v4 corner cards stay the one card style. Minimal UI also shows 19 small anchor tags: the bird's 15 (heart, ribcage, flywheel, wing, tenders, Blender, 9 export targets; tenders and mock plumes appear only within 15 units) and four for the world (Earth and Moon, Aurelsbrunn, the tether, the villagers), which carry their honest scale notes. Inside the village the bird's own tags hide.

**The tour** plays the shot list on a spline with continuous velocity (no stop at every waypoint), easing the field of view with it, and starts by flying from wherever the camera is: Establishing, Face, push-in on the eye and wattle, an orbit of the ribcage (over the head and round behind), underneath the bird and up to the tenders, the heart (watch the rings), the fan reveal (the fan unfurls), and back to the Establishing frame so the loop closes. About 85 s. When the spline reaches the Heart shot (65 s in) the tour **dives to the village**: Earth and Moon, the village from orbit, the plaza and a villager, then flies back out to the Heart shot, where the spline carries on. A slow seeded drift keeps the camera alive; flights between bird stops bow slightly off the straight line.

## What step 3 did with the planning notes

- **Scale.** The plan's note was to scale the Loom group so the ribcage radius 3.3 equals 2.4 AU. The page keeps the bird's own coordinates instead, puts Earth, the Moon and the village inside the Loom group at display sizes, and says so in the tags (see the ladder section). The 0.36 Sun stays one Sun; there is no second, true-radius Sun for a near LOD.
- **The pivot.** The `Loom` group still sways about the scene's pivot (0.72, -0.76); rather than move the heart, the page pauses the sway while the camera is at stops 4 to 7.
- **Lazy loading** is by ProximitySensor and `Inline`, as in the fan's unfurl (a ProximitySensor starting a TimeSensor); the 364 beads stay in the boot payload.
- **Still open:** the nebula sky and the red and blue streaming trails of frame D (only the gold and orange Blender streams are drawn), the eclipse stop, the flywheel growing the village from its own validated scenes, a second Sun LOD and the trailer (step 12). Music (step 10) is built, but no one has listened to it on a real device yet.

## Known rough edges

- The cloud of 2,476 stars is dense; at the ribcage stop the gems and lattice stand out from it but share the frame with it. Sorting transparent shapes against the star cloud is not controllable in plain X3D.
- The tenders and villagers are H-Anim, but their gait is a fixed cycle (no foot planting, no terrain), and what they tend or do is simulated; the wing's role colours, the shuttles and the pings of the flywheel are simulated.
- The heart beats about once a second now (varying a few percent); the tape is in the order of the run's scoreboard (by category), not a time order: the run wrote its scenes in parallel.
- Close to a feather the shadowed facets read dark navy; the plumes are alpha-blended, so overlapping blades look browner than a single one.
- Tour: the fly-through is checked against the geometry by eye at the keys, not frame by frame on a GPU.
- Frame rate on a real GPU, and the blur behind cards, are unmeasured. Nothing here quotes a software-GL frame rate: the readout in the corner of the page shows the viewer's own.
- The village's key lamp stands 55 degrees up from the south-west so that every shape keeps a shadow side; the real Heart would be straight overhead. The light is baked, so it does not flicker with the Heart's beat.
- At stops 4 and 5 the knowledge graph's beads and edges stay in the frame, because the bird's cosmos is the village's sky; it is by design, and it is the picture judge's main complaint.
- The village cap seen from orbit is a round green patch with a plaza target on a faceted Earth; it is the village, drawn 25,000 times too large for its planet, and the tag says so.
- The dive passes close to the tether mast on its way to the plaza; the villagers walk circles on three lanes and do not enter buildings; the four villagers of a lane share one body (one outfit), one gait clock and one pose, so their legs move in step and only their places on the lane and their heights (give or take 6 per cent) differ. Their light is the tender's fixed key, not the village's Cycles bake.
- The bird holds still while the camera is at stops 4 to 7 (the sway is paused), and starts swaying again from where it stopped when you fly out.
- Sound is silent until `M`, there is no button for it (the plan's four controls stay four), and the beds are AAC: any current browser decodes it, some open-source Chromium builds do not. The village bed's loop is a crossfade of two parts of a texture with a faint melody, so it can sound doubled for four seconds at the seam. The heart is synthesized, not recorded, and on small speakers only its octave above is heard. Nobody has listened to it on a real device yet.
- A comment in `tools/blender_village.py` (on `SKY_ELEV, SKY_STRENGTH`) says that a sky of 1.0 'would be about as bright as the key'; the probe measured about 2.6 times as bright (see the world pipeline). The script is left as it was, because the sha256 in `assets/village/receipt.json` pins it.
- Sprites keep the Deep scene's `PointShader`; the Sun's halos and the Blender orb use a billboard glow because the point sprite cannot grow that large cleanly.
